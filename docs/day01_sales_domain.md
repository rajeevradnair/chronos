# Day 1 — GlobalHotels Enterprise Sales Lifecycle

## Deal Snapshot

- **Customer:** GlobalHotels Corp
- **Seller:** Sony Professional Electronics
- **Opportunity ID:** OPP-812
- **Opportunity value:** approximately $3.8M–$4.0M
- **Scope:** modernization of roughly 300 conference rooms across 40 properties
- **Final outcome:** Closed Won on March 18

## Products and Services

The proposed solution includes:

- BRAVIA professional displays
- Sony PTZ cameras
- laser projectors
- integration services
- support services

## Key Stakeholders

### GlobalHotels

- **Facilities leadership** — owns the conference-room modernization initiative
- **IT / AV team** — evaluates technical requirements and product fit
- **Procurement** — negotiates pricing and commercial terms

### Sony

- **Account Executive** — owns the opportunity
- **Solutions Engineer** — validates technical requirements
- **Sales Manager** — supports commercial negotiation
- **Vice President** — approves exceptional discounting

## Core Commercial Tension

GlobalHotels is a strategically valuable multi-property customer, but the deal becomes increasingly difficult because:

1. a competitor enters with aggressive pricing,
2. GlobalHotels requests a larger discount,
3. Sony pricing policy limits normal discount authority,
4. a technical limitation is discovered before the final quote,
5. the deal requires an executive exception before it can close.

This combination of commercial, technical, policy, and customer evidence is what makes the deal useful for Chronos.

## Sales Lifecycle Timeline

### January 5 — Lead Created

GlobalHotels begins discussions with Sony about modernizing conference rooms across approximately 40 properties.

The opportunity initially centers on displays, cameras, projectors, integration, and support.

### January 12 — Opportunity Created

Sony creates opportunity **OPP-812** with an estimated value of **$3.8M**.

The deal is considered strategically important because of its size and multi-property footprint.

### January 20 — Discovery Call

GlobalHotels describes its conference-room modernization requirements.

Sony learns that:

- approximately 300 rooms are in scope,
- AV equipment must work across different room configurations,
- centralized manageability and support matter,
- commercial competitiveness will be important.

### January 25 — Quote v1

Sony submits the first formal proposal.

**Quote v1** represents Sony's initial commercial and technical understanding of the opportunity.

### February 2 — Competitor Enters

GlobalHotels informs Sony that another vendor is actively competing for the project.

Competitive pricing becomes an important risk to the opportunity.

### February 8 — Customer Requests Larger Discount

GlobalHotels asks Sony to improve pricing.

The request is driven partly by the competing offer and procurement pressure.

Sony must decide whether protecting margin or protecting the strategic opportunity is more important.

### February 12 — Pricing Policy Changes

Sony's pricing rules are updated.

Under the applicable policy:

- ordinary discount authority remains limited,
- discounts above **15% require VP approval**.

This policy becomes important when the final discount request is evaluated.

### February 15 — Quote v2

Sony produces **Quote v2** with revised commercial terms.

Negotiation continues because the revised pricing is still not sufficient to close the deal.

### February 21 — Technical Limitation Discovered

During technical validation, Sony discovers that one customer-requested capability is not currently supported by the proposed solution.

This creates a new risk:

Sales must avoid representing future capability as something available today.

The limitation becomes part of the evidence surrounding the final commercial decision.

### February 28 — Quote v3 Submitted

Sony submits **Quote v3**.

Key commercial term:

- **Discount: 17%**

The 17% discount exceeds the normal 15% threshold and therefore requires executive approval.

By this point the decision context includes:

- strategic deal value,
- competitor pricing pressure,
- customer procurement pressure,
- pricing policy,
- technical limitations,
- risk of losing the opportunity.

### March 5 — VP Approves Discount Exception

A Sony Vice President approves the 17% discount exception.

The approval allows the sales team to proceed with the commercial terms in Quote v3.

This is the primary decision Chronos will initially explain:

> Why was the 17% GlobalHotels discount approved?

### March 18 — Closed Won

GlobalHotels accepts the deal.

Opportunity **OPP-812** becomes **Closed Won**.

The final commercial outcome validates the approved pricing exception, but the later outcome must not be used as evidence when reconstructing what decision-makers knew on February 28 or March 5.

### July 10 — Previously Unavailable Capability Released

Sony releases the capability that was unavailable when the February technical limitation was discovered.

This fact is important to Chronos because it creates two different explanations:

**Historical view — February/March**

The capability was unavailable.

**Present-day view — July onward**

The capability exists.

Chronos must prevent the July information from contaminating explanations of February or March decisions.
